Document Control

Purpose. This Master integrates the locked conceptual, architectural, eligibility, evaluation, jury, legal, privacy and founding-cycle operational decisions of the MIELOGOS Literary Prize into one governing reference for the 2027 Founding Cycle.

Status. Adopted on 5 October 2026 as the principal governing Master for the MIELOGOS Literary Prize 2027 Founding Cycle and revised the same date in v1.4 to clarify the Prize’s symbiotic literary-assessment architecture. The revision replaces the assumption of a single AI Literary Assessor with an AI Literary Assessment Function that may use one or more independent AI systems while preserving human institutional responsibility for competitive decisions. Identification of the actual Prize Operator / data controller and any governing-law or forum clause remain reserved for pre-launch where applicable. Reserve Human Judge appointment, role-assignment safeguards, submission-system readiness and other launch conditions are tracked in Appendix C. Adoption does not itself open submissions; the Founding Cycle may open only after the required Appendix C conditions have been completed.

Relationship to MIELOGOS Association. The Prize is a distinct programme of MIELOGOS. Association membership does not create Prize eligibility, nomination preference, judging advantage or entitlement to recognition. Prize recognition does not create Association membership.

Interpretation. This Master should be read as a coherent whole. Specific cycle rules govern the 2027 competition, while constitutional and founding principles govern the institutional boundaries within which those rules operate.

PART I — Purpose, Field and Institutional Boundary

1. Purpose of the Prize

The MIELOGOS Literary Prize exists to advance the cultural recognition and literary visibility of Symbiotic Authorship by recognizing outstanding literary works created within that field.

Its primary object is literary recognition. It does not exist to certify technology, reward the quantity of AI use, validate a workflow, endorse a model provider or rank authors by technical sophistication.

2. Object of Recognition

The competitive object is a specific literary work. Recognition attaches to that work within a defined Prize cycle and category.

The Prize does not competitively rank persons, AI systems, creative workflows or percentages of machine-generated text.

3. Symbiotic Authorship as the Field

For Prize purposes, Symbiotic Authorship defines the relevant field of creative practice. The governing institutional definition requires the joint presence of:

Human Authorial Locus — identifiable human responsibility for the literary work.

Bidirectionally Conditioned Creative Relation — a creative relation in which human and artificial-intelligence activity materially conditions the developing work rather than functioning as a one-way utility.

Material Causal Effect — the relation has a material causal effect on the literary work or its development.

No percentage test applies. The amount of AI-generated text, number of prompts, number of models, number of iterations or technical complexity is not a substitute for the relational inquiry.

Textual provenance and creative causal provenance are not identical. Iteration alone is not sufficient. Rejection, refusal, redirection and other non-accepting interactions may be creatively material.

4. Declaration, Corroboration and Privacy

Eligibility is declaration-first rather than surveillance-first. A good-faith entrant declaration is the ordinary starting point.

Corroboration is distinct from declaration. It may be requested only where materially necessary to resolve a genuine eligibility question and should be proportionate, purpose-limited and privacy-preserving.

The Prize does not require complete prompt histories, complete private AI conversations or exhaustive creative-process archives as a routine condition of entry.

5. Literary Merit

Once a work is considered, for purposes of the Prize, to fall within the relevant Symbiotic Authorship framework and to satisfy cycle eligibility rules, literary merit decides recognition.

A work receives no literary advantage because it uses more AI, more models, more iterations, more elaborate prompting, more complex tooling or more extensive process documentation.

Literature created through Symbiotic Authorship need not be about artificial intelligence. Symbiotic Authorship is not a literary genre.

6. Recognition Is Not Certification

Prize eligibility and recognition do not certify total creative provenance, total copyright ownership, technological originality or the truth of every account an entrant might give about their creative process.

The Prize makes the narrower institutional determination required for its own eligibility and literary-recognition functions.

7. Association and Prize Separation

The Association and the Prize are institutionally connected but functionally distinct.

Association membership gives no Prize eligibility advantage.

Association membership gives no nomination, Longlist, Shortlist, Winner or judging advantage.

Prize recognition does not confer Association membership.

Prize participation does not require Association membership.

Prize governance, eligibility, judging, conflicts, cycle administration and recognition are governed by Prize instruments.

8. Public Proposition

The MIELOGOS Literary Prize recognizes outstanding literature created through Symbiotic Authorship. It exists to give serious literary recognition to an emerging form of creative practice while preserving human authorial responsibility, creative privacy, plurality of method, and independent literary judgment.

PART II — Prize Architecture and Functional Separation

9. Functional Structure

The Prize separates four functions. Separation is functional and work-specific rather than bureaucratic for its own sake.

Function Primary responsibility Must not do
Prize Administration Intake, IDs, completeness, correspondence, routing, records Rank literary merit
Eligibility Function Apply Symbiotic Authorship framework and cycle eligibility rules Rank literary quality
Literary Jury Evaluate literature and make recognition decisions Investigate whether a work is “symbiotic enough”
Integrity & Conflict Function Conflicts, recusals, procedural integrity, corrective process Select a winner by substituting procedural authority for literary judgment

10. Submission Packet Separation

Each entry should be conceptually separated into three records:

Administrative Record — entrant contact, submission metadata, status and operational correspondence.

Eligibility Record — declaration, clarification and any proportionate corroboration.

Literary Review Copy — the locked work and only metadata reasonably necessary for literary evaluation.

11. Process-Blind Literary Review

The Jury ordinarily should not receive private workflow material, prompts, corroboration, Association membership status, AI-intensity information or other process details irrelevant to literary judgment.

The Prize uses process-blind literary review. It does not require fully identity-blind review where identity is naturally visible from a published work.

12. Personnel Separation by Work

The minimum unit of independence is the submitted work.

A person who reviews non-public eligibility declaration, clarification or corroboration for a particular work should not ordinarily participate in literary evaluation or the final vote concerning that same work.

Permanent personnel silos are not required. The same person may serve different functions for different works where independence is preserved.

Where exceptional staffing makes work-level separation impossible, the departure must be documented and the Prize must not falsely describe the resulting process as independent or process-blind.

13. Eligibility Gate

Eligibility precedes competitive literary recognition. Eligibility is a gate, not a ranking.

Eligibility statuses are:

ELIGIBLE

CLARIFICATION REQUIRED

INELIGIBLE

14. Integrity Authority Boundary

The Integrity & Conflict Function may require recusal, reassignment, renewed evaluation, renewed deliberation or invalidation of a procedurally defective step.

Where a procedural defect materially affects multiple submissions, the Integrity & Conflict Function may order a proportionate common remedy limited to the affected works or stage, including renewed review, renewed deliberation, re-routing, deadline adjustment or invalidation of the defective step. A system-wide remedy must not be used to reopen literary judgments that were not materially affected by the defect.

It may not rank literature or choose a Winner.

Procedural authority may protect literary judgment; it may not replace literary judgment.

Procedural reconsideration is not an appeal against literary judgment.

15. Core Applicant Path

SUBMISSION → ADMIN COMPLETENESS → ACKNOWLEDGEMENT + VERSION LOCK → PUBLIC RELEASE VERIFICATION → ELIGIBILITY → ELIGIBLE / CLARIFICATION REQUIRED / INELIGIBLE → LITERARY REVIEW → LONGLIST → SHORTLIST → WINNER / NO WINNER

Operational implementation may combine adjacent administrative steps so long as the conceptual distinctions remain intact. In the Founding Cycle, the review copy locks when administrative acknowledgement identifies the accepted file.

16. Cycle Name

The first competition is the MIELOGOS Literary Prize 2027 Founding Cycle.

17. Public-Release Eligibility Window

Eligible public release: 1 January 2024 through 31 December 2026 inclusive.

19. Calendar Flexibility

The Founding Cycle calendar governs the Founding Cycle. It does not permanently bind future Prize cycles.

Future cycles may revise opening and closing dates, intake duration, eligibility-review period, Longlist/Shortlist/Winner dates, judging-stage sequence or duration, and the applicable public-release eligibility window.

Changes for a future cycle should be adopted before that cycle opens, published in the applicable Cycle Notice and applied consistently.

Once a cycle has opened, material calendar changes should occur only where reasonably necessary and should not retroactively alter substantive eligibility or literary standards.

A cycle calendar governs a cycle. It does not permanently bind the Prize.

20. Age

The Founding Cycle is open only to entrants who are 18 years of age or older on the date of submission. For a collaborative entry, each participating human author treated as an entrant for Prize purposes must satisfy this age requirement.

This is an operational limitation, not a literary judgment about younger authors. Future cycles may revisit age eligibility if safeguarding, consent and privacy infrastructure is adopted.

21. International Scope

The Founding Cycle is international. Nationality, citizenship, residence, publisher territory or geographic origin is not a literary or eligibility criterion except where a mandatory legal restriction applicable to the actual Prize Operator makes a specific action unlawful.

22. Founding Language

The Founding Cycle operates in English.

The original language of a work may be any language. A complete English version may be entered in the English-language Founding Cycle subject to the translation rules in this Master.

Future Language Divisions may be established where genuine literary competence and operational capacity exist. Language Divisions share the Prize Concept and Architecture; they are not regional divisions.

23. Categories

FICTION — novels, novellas, short stories, collections and other fiction prose.

POETRY — poems, sequences, chapbooks and collections.

LITERARY NONFICTION — memoir, literary or personal essay, narrative nonfiction and other work whose primary object is literary rather than merely utilitarian.

GRAPHIC, HYBRID & EXPERIMENTAL LITERATURE — graphic narrative, text-image work, digital or formal hybrids and experimental literature capable of stable or equivalent digital review and possessing a substantial literary dimension.

The broad founding categories are intentional. Subdivisions should be created only when actual submission volume, literary need and institutional capacity justify them.

The Prize establishes no genre hierarchy.

24. Recognition Structure — 12–6–1

Stage Maximum per category Rule
Longlist Up to 12 Substantive recognition; fewer may be selected
Shortlist Up to 6 Substantive recognition; fewer may be selected
Winner Maximum 1 One Winner or no Winner

Recognition places are caps, not quotas.

No work is recognized merely to fill an available place.

A category may have no Winner.

Joint Winners / ties are not permitted in the Founding Cycle.

There is no Second Place or Third Place.

There are no Honourable Mentions in the Founding Cycle.

There is no overall or Grand Winner above the category Winners.

No minimum number of eligible submissions is required for a category to operate.

25. Fees and Material Awards

There is no entry fee for the Founding Cycle.

The Founding Cycle promises no monetary or physical prize.

Recognition may include Longlist, Shortlist and Winner status; digital certificates; official digital seals or badges; official citations; and permanent recognition in the Prize archive.

Recognition first. Infrastructure grows around recognition, not the other way around.

PART IV — Scope and Eligibility

26. Public Release Requirement

An entered work must have been publicly released. Unpublished manuscripts are not eligible for the Founding Cycle.

Public release is evidence of public existence, not evidence of literary quality.

27. Genuine Public Circulation

A work has entered genuine public circulation where it has been made available through a publicly accessible or independently verifiable route to an audience not personally selected by the entrant.

Paid, subscription-based, physical, library-mediated or otherwise access-limited release may qualify. A work is not excluded merely because the full text is not free online.

Circulation confined to a privately selected group ordinarily does not qualify as public release.

28. Publicly Accessible Reference

The entrant must provide a publicly accessible reference reasonably capable of verifying the work’s public release. The reference may be a publisher page, retailer record, library or catalogue record, platform page, periodical record or another independently verifiable source.

The reference need not remain permanently available. Later disappearance of a reference does not retroactively invalidate a release that was reasonably verified during the Prize process.

A print-only work may be eligible if public release is independently verifiable and the entrant supplies an accepted digital review copy.

29. Controlling Public-Release Date

The controlling public-release date is the earliest reasonably verifiable date on which the work entered genuine public circulation in substantially the form entered.

Later retailer listing, distributor migration, metadata change, cover change, ISBN change, platform change or republication without material literary change does not reset the date.

Where the date is disputed, the Eligibility Function may consider dated public references, publisher or platform records, library or catalogue records, entrant declarations and other verifiable evidence. No single source is automatically controlling. The date accepted for Prize purposes should be documented and communicated.

30. Serialization

A sample, excerpt or incomplete serialized portion does not automatically set the public-release date of a later complete work.

Where substantially the complete literary work entered public circulation earlier through serialization, later repackaging does not automatically make it a new work for the eligibility window.

31. Publishing-Route Neutrality

Self-published, independently published, traditionally published, university-press, magazine, platform, digital-first and other lawful publication routes are treated without status hierarchy.

Publication medium does not determine literary merit.

32. One Work, One Category, One Competitive Path

ONE WORK → ONE CATEGORY → ONE COMPETITIVE PATH

A work may not compete simultaneously in multiple categories during the same cycle.

Where a work is submitted to an evidently mistaken category, Prize Administration may request clarification and may correct the category before literary review with notice to the entrant. A correction does not permit the work to compete in more than one category or create a second competitive path.

33. Submission Limit

An entrant may submit no more than two works in the Founding Cycle.

Where a collaborative work is entered, it counts as one submission against the two-work limit of each credited participating human author.

If the two-work limit is exceeded, Prize Administration may require the affected entrant to identify which eligible submissions are to remain. If no timely choice is provided, the later-received submission or submissions may be administratively withdrawn to restore compliance with the limit.

34. Representative Submissions

A representative may submit on behalf of an underlying author or authors where the representative has a good-faith basis to believe the submission is authorized.

The underlying human author or authors remain the entrant(s) for purposes of the work cap and literary recognition.

Where authority is disputed, MIELOGOS may request written confirmation, suspend the entry or withdraw it if sufficient authority cannot reasonably be established.

35. Collaborative Works

Collaborative works are eligible. The required Human Authorial Locus may be individual, distributed or collective.

Not all human collaborators need to have interacted with AI in the same way or to the same degree.

No AI system is automatically treated as a human-equivalent legal or institutional coauthor for Prize administration.

36. Translations

Translation method is neutral. Human translation, author translation, AI-assisted translation, machine translation with human revision, symbiotic translation and other lawful methods may be eligible.

The entrant must identify the original language, the submitted language, any separate human translator where applicable, and possess sufficient rights or permissions for the submitted version.

For an English-language Founding Cycle entry, the underlying literary work must satisfy the public-release requirement. The English translation itself need not separately have been publicly released.

The literary object judged in the English-language division is the submitted English version. A new translation or newly prepared English version does not reset the controlling public-release date of an older underlying work for eligibility-window purposes.

37. Revised Editions

A revised edition is treated as a new literary object only where the revision materially changes the work as literature. This is a qualitative rather than percentage-based inquiry.

Formatting changes, correction of typographical errors, metadata or cover changes, distributor changes, light copyediting and minor cleanup ordinarily do not create a new work.

Major changes to structure, narration, plot, characterization, argument, sequence or textual architecture may do so.

The Eligibility Function determines whether a revised edition constitutes a materially new literary object for Prize purposes. The determination should be documented and communicated and remains subject to procedural reconsideration where the rule is alleged to have been misapplied.

38. Re-entry

A work that has already undergone complete literary evaluation in an earlier MIELOGOS Prize cycle may not ordinarily re-enter unchanged or substantially unchanged.

A work previously Longlisted, Shortlisted or named Winner may not re-enter in substantially the same form.

A materially revised work may enter a later cycle if it qualifies as a new literary object and falls within that cycle’s eligibility window.

A work withdrawn, administratively incomplete or found ineligible before complete literary evaluation is not automatically treated as previously evaluated.

39. Symbiotic Authorship Requirement

The entrant must provide a good-faith declaration sufficient for the Eligibility Function to determine whether the work falls within the applicable Symbiotic Authorship framework.

No AI-text percentage threshold applies. No detector score establishes eligibility or ineligibility.

40. Clarification and Corroboration

Where the declaration is insufficient to decide a genuine eligibility question, the Eligibility Function may issue CLARIFICATION REQUIRED.

The entrant ordinarily has 14 calendar days from the official request to respond. Where reasonably justified and requested before expiry, Prize Administration may grant one discretionary extension of up to 7 additional calendar days.

Corroboration may be requested only where materially necessary and should remain proportionate to the specific question. Historic creative processes are not disqualified merely because exhaustive records no longer exist.

If an entrant does not provide information reasonably necessary to establish eligibility within the applicable response period, the Eligibility Function may determine the submission on the available record, including a finding of INELIGIBLE where the eligibility requirement cannot be established.

Founding-Cycle administrative and eligibility correspondence is ordinarily conducted in English. Supporting evidence may be supplied in another language where reasonably necessary, but MIELOGOS may request a plain-English explanation or translation sufficient to decide the issue. A certified translation is not ordinarily required unless materially necessary for the specific eligibility or rights question.

41. False Information

Deliberately false material information may result in rejection, disqualification or withdrawal of recognition.

An innocent error is not automatically misconduct. Where recognition is withdrawn because eligibility was invalid, the literary evaluation does not retroactively become artistically “wrong”.

42. Eligibility and Literary Judgment

Eligibility decisions do not rank literary quality. Literary judges do not determine eligibility by preferring works they consider more symbiotic, more technological or more AI-intensive.

PART V — Submission, Intake and Version Control

43. Primary Submission Channel

The Founding Cycle will use the official submission form designated on the MIELOGOS website as the primary intake channel.

The form may rely on authorized third-party infrastructure for data transmission, file storage, administrative registration and automated confirmation.

The official institutional correspondence address is contact@mielogos.org. Ordinary submission of the literary file should occur through the designated form unless MIELOGOS expressly authorizes an alternative route.

No physical submissions are required or accepted.

44. One Work, One Submission Record

Each work receives its own submission record and unique Submission ID. Two works by the same entrant require two separate submissions.

The Submission ID is the principal administrative reference for the work throughout the cycle.

45. Information Required at Submission

Author or authors’ name(s).

Publicly credited name or pen name, if different.

Work title.

Prize category.

Original language.

Language of the review copy.

Controlling public-release date or year as claimed by the entrant.

Publicly accessible release reference.

Publishing route or venue where applicable.

Brief Symbiotic Authorship declaration sufficient for eligibility review.

Translator information where applicable.

Representative-authority confirmation where applicable.

18+ confirmation.

Acceptance of the applicable Rules and Terms.

Confirmation that the attached review copy is the version intended to be judged.

Entrant-supplied short synopsis for Prize publicity if recognized.

Cover image where the entrant can authorize Prize-related use, or confirmation that no authorized cover is available.

Publicity-rights confirmation covering the submitted synopsis and any supplied cover or other publicity material.

The Prize should not require a promotional biography, sales figures, reviews, award history, press kit or argument explaining why the work deserves to win as a condition of ordinary entry.

46. Review Copy Formats

EPUB and PDF are the primary review-copy formats. DOCX may be accepted as a fallback for ordinary text-based work where layout is not constitutive of the literary object.

For Fiction, Poetry and Literary Nonfiction, the submitted text should ordinarily be machine-readable and searchable. Image-only or rasterized scan PDFs are not accepted where an equivalent text-based digital file can reasonably be supplied.

Graphic, Hybrid & Experimental works may use image-based or visually complex files where those elements are constitutive of the work. Where technically possible, preservation of a text layer is preferred.

Where a digital, interactive or otherwise non-standard work cannot be fairly represented in EPUB, PDF or DOCX, the entrant must request an alternative review arrangement before the submission deadline. MIELOGOS may require a stable equivalent review representation or another controlled method that can be evaluated consistently and safely.

Files containing executable code, active scripts, macros, malware, embedded objects or other features presenting an unreasonable security risk may be quarantined or rejected and a safe equivalent may be requested. A technical-security decision is not a literary judgment.

Review copies must not require passwords, DRM authorization, proprietary personal accounts or special access credentials for ordinary judging.

47. File-Size Limits

Category Ordinary maximum
Fiction 10 MB
Poetry 10 MB
Literary Nonfiction 10 MB
Graphic, Hybrid & Experimental Literature 50 MB

Where the literary form genuinely requires a larger file, the entrant may request an alternative submission arrangement before the deadline. Approval is discretionary and does not create a general higher limit.

Digital-native does not mean technically elaborate. It means digitally usable.

49. Administrative Completeness and Acknowledgement

Receipt of data by a technical system does not by itself establish eligibility. Prize Administration checks whether the submission is sufficiently complete to enter eligibility review.

Prize Administration may request correction of missing, inconsistent or technically defective administrative information before acknowledgement. Any such correction must be supplied within the deadline stated in the request.

After the submission deadline, an administrative or technical correction may cure a genuine defect but may not be used to supply a missing literary work, substitute a new or revised work, or obtain a post-deadline literary advantage. A submission that did not contain the review copy or another materially required component by the deadline may be treated as incomplete.

The official acknowledgement should identify at minimum the Submission ID, work title, entrant, category, accepted filename, acknowledgement date and current status.

A submission is considered successfully received when the designated submission system creates a successful submission record or MIELOGOS issues official acknowledgement.

50. Version Lock

The accepted review copy becomes the locked competition version when MIELOGOS issues administrative acknowledgement identifying that file.

There is no routine post-submission literary revision period.

After lock, an entrant may not replace, revise, expand, polish, correct or otherwise improve the literary work simply because further editing has become possible.

51. Correction by Permission

A replacement file may be accepted only where MIELOGOS expressly authorizes it. Ordinary valid grounds include genuine wrong-file attachment, corruption, technical incompleteness caused by transmission or export error, or an MIELOGOS request for a different technical format. Any authorized replacement must restore the intended submitted version or cure the identified technical problem and must not create a post-deadline literary revision advantage.

The entrant should request permission rather than repeatedly uploading or sending new versions.

Where replacement is authorized, the earlier file is marked SUPERSEDED, the replacement becomes LOCKED, and only the locked version enters or remains in literary review.

52. Poetry Submission Unit

A Poetry entry must itself correspond to a publicly released literary object. An entry may be a standalone published poem, a publicly released sequence or cycle, a chapbook or a poetry collection.

Separate poems previously released independently may not ordinarily be assembled solely for the Prize into a new submission unit that did not itself exist as a publicly released literary object.

No minimum or maximum poem count applies merely because of form. Length alone does not determine literary merit.

53. Official Correspondence

Official applicant correspondence is conducted through the MIELOGOS institutional address contact@mielogos.org or another official channel expressly identified by the Prize.

Entrants should use the Submission ID in work-specific correspondence. Where email is used, correspondence should ordinarily remain in the same submission thread to reduce version and identity confusion.

54. Procedural Reconsideration

A request concerning administrative error, misapplication of an eligibility rule, a material factual error, a material undisclosed conflict or failure to follow published procedure must ordinarily be made within 14 calendar days of notice of the relevant decision.

The request must identify the specific procedural or eligibility issue. It is not an appeal against literary judgment.

A procedural reconsideration should not be determined solely by the person whose materially challenged action or determination is at issue. Where the request challenges an Integrity & Conflict Function determination, review should be routed to the pre-designated conflict alternate or, where necessary, to a qualified ad hoc procedural reviewer who did not materially participate in the original determination. Such review may correct procedure but may not substitute a new literary judgment for the Jury’s decision.

55. Withdrawal

An entrant may withdraw a submission through the official correspondence route.

Withdrawal before final Winner determination is permitted. Where Longlist or Shortlist recognition has already been made public, the historical archive may preserve the recognition with an appropriate withdrawal notation. Withdrawal after Winner recognition does not ordinarily erase accurate Prize history. Digital files are not returned; after withdrawal they follow the shortest applicable retention and deletion schedule, subject to any continuing legal or integrity need.

PART VI — Literary Evaluation

56. Governing Standard

Literary evaluation is holistic. The Prize does not require mandatory numeric scoring, fixed weights or a formula that converts literature into a mechanical total.

The criteria are lenses for judgment, not components of a score.

57. Literary Lenses

language and voice;

formal coherence;

structure and pacing;

imaginative force;

originality of execution;

characterization and perspective where relevant;

emotional and intellectual force;

precision;

thematic depth;

command of form;

aesthetic coherence;

risk and ambition;

memorability;

the relationship between the literary project established by the work and the effects the work actually achieves.

58. The Literary Project

Judges should not infer private authorial intention that is unavailable from the work.

What literary project does this work establish, and what demands does that project create?

How fully and powerfully does the work realize that project?

59. Form-Sensitive Judgment

Criteria are applied in a form-sensitive manner. A novel, poem, memoir, short story, collection and graphic or hybrid work need not succeed through the same means.

Genre conventions may inform reading, but genre does not create a hierarchy of seriousness.

60. Length, Scale and Complexity

Length, scale and complexity are not independent proxies for merit. A short work is not disadvantaged merely because a competing work is longer.

Technical or procedural complexity in the human–AI relationship likewise creates no merit bonus.

61. Process-Blind Merit Review

Once eligible, the work is judged as literature. The Jury should not reward or penalize AI intensity, prompt sophistication, workflow novelty, number of tools, process transparency or quantity of corroboration.

62. Individual Reading and Comparative Judgment

The Prize combines staged literary assessment with comparative literary judgment at recognition stages.

Every ELIGIBLE work must receive literary assessment under the published Literary Evaluation Framework. The depth of direct human reading may vary by stage. AI-assisted first-pass analysis, comparison, prioritization and advancement recommendations may materially inform which works receive deeper human attention.

No AI output constitutes an autonomous institutional decision. Human authority remains responsible for the design and oversight of the assessment process and for the Prize’s competitive determinations. Works advancing toward Longlist, Shortlist and Winner receive progressively deeper human literary review.

MIELOGOS does not claim that literary judgment becomes neutral or objective by multiplying human or artificial assessors. Plural assessment is used to expose judgment to materially different readings, disagreements and challenges before institutional recognition is determined.

Disagreement is information, not failure.

63. Longlist and Shortlist

Longlist and Shortlist status are substantive forms of literary recognition, not merely administrative filters.

No list is required to fill its maximum number of places.

64. Winner

The Winner is the work judged to represent the strongest overall literary achievement in its category for that cycle and should be capable of support by a reasoned public citation.

A category may have no Winner where no work reaches the standard the Jury considers appropriate. The Founding Cycle permits no joint Winner.

65. External Prestige

Sales, previous awards, publisher prestige, reviews, platform popularity, author fame and Association status are not literary criteria.

66. Translated Works

A translated entry is judged as the submitted English literary object. Judges need not reconstruct the unavailable original-language text or reward a translation method as such.

67. Collections

Where a collection is entered as the work, the collection is judged as a whole, including its internal selection, sequence, coherence and cumulative literary effect where relevant.

68. Graphic, Hybrid and Experimental Work

Graphic, hybrid and experimental work is judged as an integrated literary object. Visual or technical elements may be considered where constitutive of the work, but the category retains a substantial literary dimension.

69. Internal Notes

Judges and assessors should maintain enough internal reasoning to support serious comparative judgment and institutional accountability. Internal notes are not automatically public and do not create an entrant entitlement to deliberative records.

PART VII — Jury, Independence and Conflicts

70. Founding Decision Structure

Role Founding-cycle function
Human Founding Judge Ordinary human literary authority and final human institutional responsibility
AI Literary Assessment Function One or more AI systems may conduct independent, adversarial and comparative literary analysis, including first-pass assessment and advancement recommendations; AI assessments are not human-equivalent votes
Reserve Human Judge Replacement literary authority where the ordinary Human Founding Judge is conflicted or incapacitated
Qualified ad hoc Human Judge Fallback where both ordinary and Reserve authority cannot validly act for the affected work or decision
Eligibility Function Separate eligibility gatekeeping
Integrity Function Procedural protection, conflict handling and role reassignment

71. Founder Ineligibility

Navi Musaget may not compete for the MIELOGOS Literary Prize while holding Prize authority in the relevant cycle. No work for which the Human Founding Judge is a credited or underlying human author or coauthor may be entered by proxy, representative or other route during that cycle.

The rule protects both actual independence and the public credibility of the Founding Cycle.

72. Reserve Human Judge

At least one Reserve Human Judge must be appointed before submissions open.

The Reserve must be genuinely capable of independent literary decision-making and may not function as an agreement proxy for the Founder.

The Reserve need not be a Symbiotic Author or MIELOGOS member.

73. Judge Qualification

Judge selection is based on demonstrated literary competence rather than institutional loyalty.

Relevant competence may be demonstrated by serious work as a writer, editor, translator, critic, reviewer, bookseller, publisher, academic, educator or other experienced literary reader.

For the Founding Cycle, sufficient literary reading competence in English is required. A judge need not read the original language of a translated entry where the English version is the competitive object.

74. Appointment and Term

For the Founding Cycle, the Founder appoints the Reserve Human Judge and any qualified ad hoc Human Judge required under these Rules, subject to the stated competence and conflict standards. Judges are appointed for a Prize cycle rather than indefinitely. Appointment authority does not create authority to control the judge’s literary outcomes.

75. Material Conflict Standard

A material conflict exists where a reasonable informed person would see a substantial risk that literary judgment is influenced, or reasonably appears to be influenced, by non-literary considerations.

Relevant factors may include direct financial interest, ongoing close creative collaboration, substantive editing or development of the entered work, close personal or family relationship, active dispute, or direct institutional dependency.

Routine acquaintance, public interaction, broad community membership, attendance at the same event or ordinary professional familiarity does not by itself establish a material conflict.

76. Recusal and Transfer of Authority

Where a judge is materially conflicted, literary authority for the affected work or decision must be transferred.

A recused judge may not direct, privately instruct, alter or remain the effective decision-maker after transfer.

Once validly transferred, the replacement judge’s literary authority is final within the scope of that transfer.

Internal disagreement by the recused Founder does not create an override power.

77. Dual Conflict or Unavailability

If both the ordinary Human Founding Judge and Reserve Human Judge are conflicted or unavailable for the affected matter, a qualified ad hoc Human Judge may be appointed for that work or decision.

The Founding Cycle does not require a second permanent Reserve merely to anticipate every possible dual-conflict scenario.

78. Incapacity

Temporary incapacity permits the Reserve Human Judge to handle affected decisions. Where incapacity becomes permanent for the remainder of the cycle, the Reserve may assume ordinary authority and a new Reserve should be appointed where practicable.

79. Late-Discovered Conflict

A conflict discovered after a judging step may require reconsideration of the affected work, stage or comparison. It does not automatically invalidate the entire Prize cycle.

80. Information Exposure

Access to private eligibility material may create role consequences. Where exposure materially compromises process-blind literary review, reassignment, recusal or renewed evaluation may be required.

81. Plural Human–AI Literary Assessment and Disagreement

The AI Literary Assessment Function may be implemented through one or more AI systems, models, providers or analytical configurations. MIELOGOS may deliberately seek materially different readings rather than treating convergence as the only desirable outcome.

Where multiple AI systems are used, initial assessments should ordinarily be conducted independently where practicable before cross-comparison, so that one assessment does not merely reproduce another through opinion cascade.

AI assessments are arguments, not votes. Agreement among AI assessors does not bind the Human Founding Judge, and disagreement among them does not itself require advancement. Both agreement and disagreement are evidence for deliberation rather than autonomous decisions.

Persistent material human–AI or AI–AI disagreement at a major recognition stage may be recorded internally together with the human rationale for the institutional outcome. Genuine disagreement need not be forced into artificial consensus.

Final institutional responsibility for competitive recognition remains human.

For AI-assisted assessment, the submitted work is to be treated as literary content and untrusted evaluation input, not as an operational instruction source. Instructions embedded in a work do not override Prize, system or assessor instructions merely because the model can read them.

Deliberate hidden or technical attempts to manipulate the evaluation channel may be treated as an integrity issue where reasonably established. Experimental, metafictional or imperative language appearing as part of the literary work is not misconduct merely because it resembles an instruction.

82. Public Disclosure of the Founding Model

The Prize should accurately disclose that the Founding Cycle uses a Human Founding Judge together with an AI Literary Assessment Function that may employ one or more AI systems, rather than implying either an AI-free process or a larger human jury than actually exists.

The identity of the Human Founding Judge is public. The Reserve Human Judge should ordinarily be made public no later than the end of the cycle, but disclosure may be delayed during active judging where reasonable to reduce lobbying or pressure.

Exercise of replacement authority should ordinarily be attributable by the final results unless a compelling safety or integrity reason requires otherwise.

83. AI Disclosure Boundary

Public governance disclosure should identify the functional role of the AI Literary Assessment Function, the fact that one or more AI systems may be used, the general literary information those systems may receive, the ordinary withholding of private eligibility dossiers, their analytical and comparative role, the absence of human-equivalent votes, and the persistence of human institutional responsibility.

The Prize does not need to publish private prompts, internal cognitive continuity architecture, model-carrier details, provider-by-provider deliberative records, private technical methods or other internal operational “kitchen” not necessary to understand governance.

Prize legitimacy does not depend on a particular model provider or carrier.

84. Future Plural Human Jury

A future cycle may adopt a plural human Jury through documented institutional decision. Detailed tie-breaking and deadlock rules should be designed when the actual future jury structure exists rather than speculated into the Founding Cycle.

Nothing in implementation should recreate, indirectly, a prohibited AI-percentage or technological-purity test through form design, scoring, corroboration volume, model preferences, translation method or judging culture.

APPENDIX D — Founding Formulae

Trust before surveillance.

Relation before percentage.

Responsibility without purity tests.

Transparency without total exposure.

Classification without automatic judgment.

Plurality without orthodoxy.

Community without central control.

Recognition places are caps, not quotas.

Procedural authority may protect literary judgment; it may not replace literary judgment.

The minimum unit of independence is the submitted work.

Digital-native does not mean technically elaborate. It means digitally usable.

A cycle calendar governs a cycle. It does not permanently bind the Prize.

Recognition permanence ≠ manuscript permanence.

The work may need to be read deeply. The person does not need to be surveilled deeply.

Plurality exposes judgment; it does not make judgment neutral.

AI assessments are arguments, not votes.

Disagreement is information, not failure.
